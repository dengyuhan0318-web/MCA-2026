Discussion and reflection

This was my first experience of using MuseScore Studio for music transcription. Its notation view resembles a printed score, so I found it easy to compare the notes entered in MuseScore Studio with those in the source PDF. Its menus are straightforward to navigate, making it easy to perform basic notation tasks, such as selecting note durations, entering pitches and adding rests. Furthermore, the playback function helped me identify obvious pitch and rhythmic errors. However, details such as dynamics, slurs and accidentals required more time to adjust. I found it particularly easy to make mistakes when adding slurs in passages with more complex notation.

Moreover, I recognise that whilst a transcription may sound like the original piece, this does not mean it has fully retained every detail of the original score. This is because even if the pitch and rhythm are correct, incorrect slurs may still affect the reader’s perception of the music. Therefore, listening to the piece can be a method of verification, but should not be the sole basis for judgement. I shall check transcriptions in two stages: by listening to verify and then by comparing each bar against the PDF. I will also pay attention to the starting and ending points of each slur. This will ensure that the transcription aligns more closely with the edition of the score I have chosen.
