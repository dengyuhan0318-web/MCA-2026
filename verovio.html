Task 2: Comparison of elements in MusicXML and MEI files

1. Note: <note> → <note>

Both MusicXML and MEI use <note> to represent notes, but they are organised differently. MusicXML places pitch and duration in child elements such as pitch, duration and type, and uses attributes such as id, default-x and default-y for identification and positioning; MEI, on the other hand, uses attributes such as pname, oct and dur to represent note names, octaves and durations. In this example, MusicXML notes are placed directly in the measure, whilst MEI notes are placed in a beam inside a layer within a staff, or may also be placed directly in a layer or chord. For the first B4 sixteenth note, MusicXML uses duration=1 in conjunction with divisions=4 to specify the duration, whilst MEI uses dur="16". Consequently, when we need to modify the pitch, we should change the child elements in MusicXML, whereas in MEI we should modify the attributes. In MusicXML, rests are contained in the note element, whilst in MEI a separate rest element is used.

<note default-x="82.99" default-y="-20">
  <pitch><step>B</step><octave>4</octave></pitch>
  <duration>1</duration><voice>1</voice><type>16th</type>
  <stem>up</stem><staff>1</staff>
  <!-- beam and notations omitted -->
</note>
<note xml:id="l3xgubk" dur.ppq="1" dur="16"
      oct="4" pname="b" stem.dir="up" />

2. Measure: <measure> → <measure>

Both MusicXML and MEI use <measure> to represent a bar, but their internal structures are not entirely the same. In the MusicXML example, the bar is contained in a part and must have a number attribute. It may also utilise attributes such as implicit, width and id, and its child elements include note, attributes, direction, backup and barline. In this example, the MEI bar is located in a section; MEI also permits measures within an ending. It can utilise attributes such as n, xml:id, metcon and left. Its child elements include staff, mNum and control events such as slur, whilst notes are located in the nested staff/layer structure. MEI organises simultaneous sounds using staves and layer containers, whereas MusicXML organises music through voice and staff values and timing movements such as backup.

<part id="P1">
  <measure number="0" implicit="yes" width="187.36">
    <!-- attributes, notes, direction and backup -->
  </measure>
</part>
<section xml:id="u1p8nga7">
  <measure xml:id="s1nf4hxw" metcon="false" n="0">
    <mNum xml:id="b1t0sw0" />
    <staff n="1"><layer n="1"><!-- events --></layer></staff>
    <staff n="2"><layer n="5"><!-- events --></layer></staff>
    <!-- dynamics, tempo, slur and fingering omitted -->
  </measure>
</section>

3. Slur: <slur> → <slur>

Both MusicXML and MEI use the <slur> element to represent a slur, but they differ in how they handle the endpoints. MusicXML typically places the slur in the notations of the endpoint notes, using the mandatory type attribute to indicate a start, stop or continuation, and employing the number attribute to distinguish between overlapping slurs. It may also use attributes such as placement, bezier-x and bezier-y to describe the position and curve shape, but the element itself has no child elements. In this example, MEI places a slur element in a measure, using startid and endid to reference the two endpoint notes; timestamps may also be used to specify the endpoints. MEI employs attributes such as curvedir, bezier and bulge to describe the curve, and permits the inclusion of a curve child element. Consequently, MusicXML must ensure that start and stop markers are correctly paired, whilst MEI must ensure that endpoint ID references are valid. When note IDs are modified, the corresponding references must also be updated.

<notations>
  <slur type="start" bezier-x="23.945686"
        bezier-y="13.764924" number="1" />
</notations>
<!-- on the endpoint note: -->
<notations><slur type="stop" number="1" /></notations>
<slur xml:id="m8v21cg" startid="#l3xgubk" endid="#j18ftah4" />
