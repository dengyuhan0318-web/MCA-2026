## Topic

The topic I have chosen for my Music Curation and Analysis project is the composer Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. I chose Mozart because when I was young, I played many of his piano works. As a result, I am familiar with his musical style and piano compositions, and I have long admired his elegant and expressive compositional style. Although Mozart’s body of work is extremely wide-ranging, including symphonies and operas, in this project I shall focus primarily on his piano sonatas and related piano works. For example, in the subsequent MuseScore task, I plan to use the piano version of Rondo Alla Turca.

## Challenges and Current Data Manifestations

### Challenges:

1. **The same musical work can exist in many different versions**. A piece may have an original manuscript, several printed editions, modern digital scores and many audio recordings. About Mozart, the same piano sonata may be performed by many different pianists, and each performance can differ in tempo, intensity and interpretation. Therefore, it is difficult to decide which version should be treated as the main or representative version in a dataset.
2. **Music data is often distributed across different platforms**. Scores, metadata, and recordings are not always stored in the same archive. For Mozart, for example, the Digital Mozart Edition provides detailed information and digital scores, while IMSLP may provide other score editions and some recordings. This means that it takes a great deal of time to collect data from different platforms.
3. Another challenge is **copyright and access**. Mozart’s compositions themselves are in the public domain, but some modern editions and recent recordings may still be protected by copyright. Therefore, materials may be visible online but not always freely reusable.

### Current Data Manifestations:

For my selected theme, Mozart, there is already a large amount of well-curated digital data. Resources such as the **Digital Mozart Edition** collect and organise digital scores together with metadata such as titles, Köchel catalogue numbers and genre. Other platforms such as **IMSLP** and **MuseScore** provide scanned scores and different editions. In addition, Video recordings of Mozart’s works are widely available on YouTube.

These resources usually present music through searchable databases. Users can search by work title, catalogue number or sometimes performer. Scores are commonly presented as scanned pages or digital notation, while metadata is shown as structured descriptive information. Video recordings are usually presented as separate performance files linked to the work.

As a result, Mozart’s music is highly accessible and relatively well organised. Digital archives also make it easier for users to compare and access them across platforms than in the past. However, there are also weaknesses. The relationship between scores, metadata, and recordings is not always clearly connected. Besides, having many editions and recordings may increase the difficulty of users’ choices. For those compiling datasets, it remains necessary to establish a relatively uniform set of selection criteria and use stable identifiers such as Köchel numbers to link different types of data.
